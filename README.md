## Niels Karel Snooij

I want the sea and the soil to still be worth looking at in fifty years.

That is the whole reason I do this. Most of what lives in a litre of seawater
has no name. Deeper down, almost none of it does. We are losing species we never
described, and you cannot protect what you cannot count. So I build small models
that read DNA and can say "this looks like nothing I know" without knowing what
it is instead.

The second half is health. The molecules a soil bacterium makes to defend itself
are where most of our antibiotics came from. Thousands of those genes sit in
public genomes and nobody has ever switched them on. I find them, work out what
the product should weigh, and give them to laboratories that can grow them.

I measure everything against the simplest method that could give the same
answer. Usually that method is counting, and often counting wins. I publish that
too.

### What is here

**[EH14 X](https://github.com/bioxcompute/eh14x)** reads marker genes and
environmental DNA. It tells a real read from a PCR artefact better than the tool
people use for it, 0.9735 against 0.9442 on the same set.

**[dark-taxa-novelty](https://github.com/bioxcompute/dark-taxa-novelty)** is the
benchmark, with the baseline in one numpy file so you can beat it yourself.

Nothing I have designed has been in a laboratory yet. When it has, the result
goes up either way.
